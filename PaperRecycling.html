Paper recycling and spreading awareness of North Macedonia's issue with waste management

Тhe issue behind the motivation

According to the State Statistical Office of North Macedonia 99.8% of the municipal waste ends up in landfills.

In the years 2020-2025 the largest part of this waste - 84%, was classified as “mixed municipal waste” which contains commercial waste and whose composition may be various, therefore unknown. Additionally, the Ministry of Environment and Physical Planning estimates 2000 illegal landfills (while the real number is possibly higher), and more than 50 landfills which do not meet waste management standards. All of these, whether legal or not, are dealt with in the same way: by incineration. This is oftentimes the reason for wildfires in the summer months which not only pollute the air around settlements but also spread to the forests and threaten wildlife in the vicinity.

Moreover, due to a lack of enforcement of regulations surrounding environmental protection, industrial waste containing hazardous chemicals often ends up released in nature. Import of waste from other countries further worsens the waste (mis)management in the country, while a uniform and established waste selection and recycling infrastructure is virtually non-existent. Efforts are being made by non-governmental associations such as Pakomak – a non-profit company comprised of some of the leading food and beverage manufacturing companies in the country which collaborates with collection, storage and recycling companies, thus providing organization of packaging waste management to manufacturers, importers or even municipalities [1].
