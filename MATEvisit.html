Visiting MATE: synthetic seeds

Nov 2025

As part of the curricular activities of the 2023 Biotechnology cohort of the University of Pécs I visited the Hungarian University of Agriculture and Life Sciences (MATE), which, besides other activities, conducts research into agricultural production focusing on natural resource management and environmental protection.

We had the opportunity to visit the facilities in Gödöllő where one of the research groups is working on making synthetic seeds (synseeds) from nodal segments. A synseed is an artificial endosperm – a hydrogel made of calcium chloride (CaCl2) and sodium alginate in MS media (3% sucrose) that encapsulates the embryo - in this case shoot meristem, axillary buds, somatic embryos, or other regenerative explants, and provides nutrients, moisture and protection. These artificial seeds can be useful for conservation of endangered plants, propagating plants with seedless fruit, low seed viability or poor germination.

I had the chance to create some synseeds. Briefly, we first cut 7-8mm nodal segments and transferred them into the sodium alginate solution, then the tip of a pipette is cut with a sterilized knife and used to aspirate a single nodule with some of the sodium alginate solution, which is then dispensed in the CaCl2 solution. The drop turns into a transparent semi-solid sphere because of the ion-exchange reaction between the carboxylate groups in alginate and the calcium ions, thus encapsulating the nodule. These synseeds can then germinate in soil or in MS media – as seen in the examples below previously made by the research group in MATE.
